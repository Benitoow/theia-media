# Theia 3.3.5: the player's settings carry the film, and it measures itself

On Windows x64, this is still your one file:

**[Download Theia 3.3.5 for Windows x64](https://github.com/Benitoow/theia-media/releases/download/v3.3.5/theia-setup-windows-amd64.exe)**

On a Mac with Apple Silicon:

**[Download Theia 3.3.5 for macOS Apple Silicon](https://github.com/Benitoow/theia-media/releases/download/v3.3.5/theia-setup-darwin-arm64)**

Both install the server, the native player, the `theia` command and the pinned
video engine, verify every downloaded byte against GitHub's SHA-256 digest, and
put the installation on your `PATH`. To install without a network connection, use
the offline bundle for
**[Windows](https://github.com/Benitoow/theia-media/releases/download/v3.3.5/theia-3.3.5-windows-amd64.zip)**
or
**[macOS](https://github.com/Benitoow/theia-media/releases/download/v3.3.5/theia-3.3.5-darwin-arm64.zip)**
instead: unpack it and run `theia-setup` or `theia-setup.exe` from inside the
folder.

Everything else under *Assets* is a component consumed by the installer or the
automatic updater. There are fourteen of them, unchanged since 3.3.4.

## What changed

- **The player's settings are about the film now, not only about the shell.**
  The sheet's playback section held two switches about the *screen* - the
  controls' three seconds and the reduced motion - and nothing about what
  plays. Those two moved to **Interface**, where they belong, and the sheet has
  **Lecture** (play the next episode automatically, the audio language asked
  for, the subtitles asked for) and a new **Sous-titres** section: a preview and
  seven decisions - size, height, colour, outline, the band behind the text, the
  family and the weight - with one reset. The unit is a pixel **at 1080** in all
  three places that matter: the two sliders, the sentence under them, and Rust.
  The preview is not a picture of a subtitle, it is the same arithmetic drawn, so
  a size the sheet shows is the size the film takes.
- **Four engine facts behind that section were measured rather than assumed.**
  `sub-margin-y` is ignored outright on a track that brings its own ASS script -
  a `MarginV=200` script put its text 207 px up while the margin asked for 80,
  even under `sub-ass-override=force` - so the position is `sub-pos`, with
  `sub-ass-force-style=MarginV=0` to let it through; the engine's default
  override kept the script's own font, size and colour (27 px white Arial where
  the sheet asked for bone at 24), so the sheet uses `force`; and the engine has
  no borderless style at all, so "no outline" is a transparent fitted box while a
  band behind the text is the full-width television band, at 70 % alpha.
- **The player no longer sends a frame nobody needs, and it measures itself.**
  It emitted its status twice a second forever and the interface re-rendered on
  every one: an idle player used 2.8 % of a core for a screen that said the same
  thing. It now emits when the state a viewer can see has moved, plus a two
  second heartbeat - **measured after: 0.9 %**, the same memory, the picture
  untouched. The interface measures its own frames with a sampler armed by a
  gesture and stopped after three seconds, reporting the worst frame interval,
  the frames past two at 60 Hz, and how many frames it drew, so that "zero
  milliseconds" and "never ran" cannot be confused; the engine's own `fps`,
  `drops` and `decoderDrops`, always in mpv and never asked for, travel with it.
- **Resizing the window stopped re-making the window region per pixel.** The
  region is `SetWindowRgn`, and re-making it on every resize event made a drag of
  the window's edge lag - reproduced with a scripted drag of forty steps, the OSD
  drew **five** frames. The region is dropped once when a drag starts and put
  back once the size has settled: **ninety-five frames, worst interval 8.8 ms**.
  What remains is honest and not fixed: about 12 ms per step inside mpv and about
  19 ms of message-loop and composition latency around it.
- **What was watched is a dashboard, in the web interface and in the player.**
  Hours of films and hours of series for the profile this machine is set to,
  films and series started and watched, the films/series ratio as a split bar, and
  the series ranked by time watched with their TMDB posters and a rule showing how
  far into each one the viewer is. Two tiles a reference asked for are absent
  **on purpose**: a per-session average and a streak would need to know *when*
  things were watched, and this schema keeps one row per item and no history.
- **The player's menu has two tabs, and the volume is a slider.** *Langues* first
  - the question asked mid-film - and *Qualité* behind it, with no row repeated
  in both. The ladder is the server's own: a rung is a reload of the converted
  route rather than a query on the file, because a pipe has no ranges to seek in;
  the file's own rung stays the default, is the one the menu leads with, and is
  the only one that reaches an amplifier untouched; and the rungs that need an
  encoder are greyed when every slot is taken.

## Verification boundary

Everything below was run for this release, on this machine.

- The render check drives the real interface in Chromium: the sheet's six
  sections, the subtitle pane's arithmetic read back **out of the page** at the
  defaults and after both sliders move (the preview stands for a picture 1080
  tall, so `px × stage / 1080` is a number the check can compute), the value
  beside each slider, the outline inverting over dark text, three outlines that
  differ from each other, the band at seventy per cent, the family, the weight,
  a reset link that is grey until something has changed, and the same object
  reaching the engine when the sheet is saved.
- The **engine itself**, measured: a throwaway probe loads the pinned
  `libmpv-2.dll` headless, sets `vo=image`, renders a real 1280x720 frame carrying
  a real `.srt` and a real `.ass`, and reads the pixels. That is where the three
  facts above come from, including the numbers 40 px → 31, 120 px → 84, 200 px →
  137 on a 720-tall picture, identical for both kinds of track.
- The program a viewer runs: the release build was installed on the maintainer's
  machine and its own `--diagnostics` printed what the engine holds -
  `asked 36px at 120px, Shadow, band none, Standard, normal | sub-font-size=24.000000
  sub-pos=88.889999 sub-margin-y=0 sub-color=#FFEDE7DC sub-border-style=outline-and-shadow
  sub-border-size=0.000000 sub-shadow-offset=1.200000 sub-font=sans-serif
  sub-bold=no sub-ass-override=force sub-ass-force-style=MarginV=0` - so the chain
  the sheet promises, page to command to engine, is measured in the program
  rather than only in the tree.
- Rust: `cargo check` clean with no warning, and `cargo test` 30/30 including
  fourteen tests over the mapping's arithmetic. Go: `go vet ./...` clean and
  `go test ./...` green. The interface guard, the web suite at 375, 1280 and 1920,
  and the playback guard across four browsers, as the release pipeline runs them.
- The macOS assets are built and verified by the pipeline's `macos-14` jobs
  exactly as they were for 3.3.4 - the bundle, the archive, and
  `scripts/verify-macos.sh`. **Nothing was run on a Mac for this release's
  changes**, and this record does not claim otherwise: the interface is the same
  source, and it is not a Mac that was looked at.

**What is not verified, and is not claimed.** Three things need a person, and the
first two are the whole point of this release:

- **How the subtitles look over a moving film.** The preview is arithmetic, not a
  photograph, and the sheet's promise is about the film. Gestures G25 and G27 of
  `player/PROTOCOL.md` are that check, and they want the maintainer's own screen
  and his own library - including the ASS case, which is what disc remuxes carry.
- **The next episode starting by itself.** The guard, the end-of-file reading and
  the load path are unit-tested and written down, but nothing here plays an
  episode to its end: gesture G26.
- **Whether the resize now feels settled** to the person who reported the lag.
  The numbers say 95 frames and an 8.8 ms worst interval where there were 5
  frames, and the remaining cost per step is written down rather than fixed.
