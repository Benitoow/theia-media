# Theia 5.0 — everything you own, in one library

**The biggest update Theia has ever had.**

Theia 5 is the first release of the V5 generation. Your computers, your NAS and
your cloud become one library, with one profile that follows you from one server
to the other, on the desktop, on your phone and on your sofa. The web administration,
the native player and the installer all speak the same settings and the same sources.

5.0 also brings an **Android application** for your phone, and a **beta** of the same
application on Android TV and Google TV.

The server, the library and ordinary viewing remain free for noncommercial use.
There is no cloud account and no telemetry.

This page says what was checked on which system, and what was not. Windows is
checked first.

## What arrives with 5.0

### One library, many places

Films, Series, Search and Home combine the folders of several Theia servers, the
NAS folders mounted on them and cloud folders. A small line under each title says
where the copy lives. There is no separate room to switch to: the sources are the
library, and a source that is asleep or revoked leaves its known titles visible
while the others keep playing.

### One profile that follows you

Link the profile you use on one server to the profile you use on another. What you
have watched and where you stopped follow you from server to server, with a
documented rule for conflicts, rewatches and resets. Two households keep their own
profiles: the link is explicit and can be undone without deleting history.

### Google Drive as a source

Connect Google Drive and read the folders you choose, in short bounded pieces, so a
5 TB library does not have to be downloaded to be browsed. The Drive copy joins the
same title page as a local one: play, resume, mark as watched, leave an opinion and
preview a scene all work on it.

**Until Google has approved Theia's own Google connection, Drive may ask for your
own Google client** (the application creation is guided in the settings page).
Google's approval is a separate process from this release.

### NAS folders

A share mounted on a server joins the library like a local folder, and your history
survives the machine going to sleep. The UGREEN test kit has not been checked on a
real UGREEN yet; a mounted share that this system can read is what 5.0 supports.

### Servers and sources, in settings

Add, rename, reconnect and remove servers and sources on one settings page, in the
player's **Server & sources** and in the web administration, each with a small icon:
tower, laptop, NAS or cloud. Pair a machine with a short code, change a source's
icon by hand, and see whether it is reachable, asleep or refused.

### A friend request shows who it is

Enter a friend code and the person's name and picture appear straight away - even
if their computer is off - while the request waits for them to accept. On their
side it arrives with your name and your picture too. A profile already publishes
those two things for friends; showing them before acceptance changes nothing else,
and nothing about their library, their viewing or their opinions is readable until
they accept.

### The connect screen remembers your servers

The connect screen keeps the list of servers you have already joined: one click
and you are back, without retyping an address. Network discovery stays right
there and reports what it finds; the address field is only for a fallback. In the
background, an animated glyph field drawn by the page itself - no external image
added - and a clean still frame if you requested reduced motion. The cold start
uses the same card and the same promise, with a breathing brass glow behind the
title and three quiet dots beside the line; connecting to a server shows a
spinner while it answers.

### One page for every title

The same title page whichever source holds the file: play or resume it, mark it
watched or reset it, leave an opinion among friends, and preview a scene - with the
exact copy you chose, never a substitute from another source.

### Watched means watched

Anti-spoil reveals an episode's artwork once you pass its last fifth, and a file's
own ending-credits chapter counts as watched. A file without such a chapter keeps
the older thresholds, and *Mark as watched* is always there.

### An update that keeps everything

Upgrading from 4.4.6 keeps your library, profiles, watchlists and viewing history.
Checked on a real 4.4.6 installation, including a rollback.

### The pointer leaves the picture

While a film plays and the controls have gone, the mouse pointer goes with them
(the connect screen, the library, a paused film and a woken player keep it).
Measured in the real player window on Windows 11: hidden in 20 of 20 samples on two
rest cycles of a playing film, and one pixel of mouse movement brings both back.

### Hold the play key to run at twice the speed

Hold the play/pause key (Space by default) on a playing film: it runs at 2× while
the key stays down, a small `2×` badge says so, and letting go returns to normal.
A short press is still play/pause. It does not apply in a watch party, which keeps
one speed for everybody.

### Your library on your phone

An Android application connects to your Theia server and plays your library on the
phone: Home, Films, Series and Search, the same title page, your profile and the
places where you stopped. Start a film on the desktop and carry on from the phone, or
the other way round.

It is drawn for the hand rather than shrunk from the desktop: no window, the controls
over the picture, a double tap to skip, a timeline you drag, a lock against stray
touches, and a pinch to fill the screen or show the whole picture. Leaving the
application during a film opens picture-in-picture; the notification and the lock screen
control it. Subtitles and the audio track are yours to
choose, and a file the phone cannot open as it is, such as an MKV, is asked of your
server in a form it can.

It needs a Theia server it can reach on your network: type its address, or use *Find a
server*. Away from home is not covered by this release.

**Install it** from the release page: download `theia-android-5.0.0.apk`, allow the
installation when Android asks, and open it. It needs Android 8.0 or later. It is
signed with the maintainer's own key, is not on Google Play, and does not update
itself: to update, install the next APK over it.

### Android TV and Google TV (beta)

The same application installs on an Android TV or a Google TV and opens a television
presentation of Theia: browse and play with the remote's arrows, OK and Back, with the
interface sized for the sofa and your remote's Back leaving one screen at a time. It
declares itself to the television as a television application, so it should appear
among your apps.

When your television or receiver can take AC-3, E-AC-3 or DTS, the sound is kept as it
is instead of being turned into stereo; otherwise you get stereo, as before. TrueHD is
always stereo for now.

**This is a beta.** Expect rough edges, and tell us what you find.

## Updating to Theia 5

**Run the 5.0 setup once**, including from 4.4.6, or use the update button.
This is a **shell release**: the native player changes (the playback speed the hold
gesture needs), and updating the server or an interface pack alone cannot deliver
it. The setup replaces the desktop programs and keeps your library, profiles,
watchlists, preferences and viewing history.

Earlier compatible signed interface packs remain supported. This release carries
its interface inside the player; there is no separate interface-pack download for
5.0.

**Player updates are your choice.** The player does not check for updates on its
own: open *Settings → Updates* and use **Check for updates**, or switch on
**Automatic updates** so it looks when it starts and offers to restart. A server
installed alone still updates itself, and never during a film.

**The Android application does not update itself.** Install the next `theia-android` APK
over the old one; it keeps your server and your choices. It is signed by the same key
each time, which is what lets Android accept it.

## Not in 5.0

- **A phone that reads your NAS or Drive without a server** (an autonomous Android
  mode) is not built.
- **Casting**, Google TV's home rows and the phone as a remote are not part of this
  release.
- **Native Windows and Linux ARM64 applications** are not part of this release;
  older ARM64 setups stay labelled as earlier versions.
- **Offline downloads** remain on hold.

## What was checked, and what was not

Checked on Windows 11 on the maintainer's machine, with disposable servers and
data, generated test media and the pinned engines: the one-library composition
over loopback servers, the linked profile and its conflict rules, Google Drive
with a real account (two videos recognised), the NAS mounted-path behaviour
(offline, reconnect, history), the title page across a local, a paired and a Drive
copy, the settings surfaces in the player and the web administration, the typography
roles, the upgrade from 4.4.6 with rollback, the pointer rule in the real player
window, the hold-to-run gesture in the interface benches and the shell's own
tests, and the Android application on one physical phone (a Xiaomi POCO on Android
16) against disposable servers: the connected library, real films and episodes
played through the server, the touch controls, the notification and the lock screen,
picture-in-picture, resuming between the phone and the desktop in both directions,
and the profile photo picker.

Not checked: a second household or a second physical machine on a real network; a
real UGREEN; a physical Mac or Linux machine; any other phone or Android version
than the one above; a telephone call during a film; installing under Google's
developer verification; HDR output through an amplifier; and Google's approval of
Theia's own Drive application. No recorded viewing has run a real film at 2× yet:
the gesture is covered by the interface's own behaviour checks, and what it does in
your films is exactly the kind of report the next update will answer. The Google Drive source was exercised with the
maintainer's own client and a real account. HDR is not tonemapped on a composed
(remote or cloud) copy in this release.

Field reports from real households are what the next updates will be shaped by.

## External services

The only external services are **TMDB** for film and series metadata and artwork,
**GitHub Releases** for updates and the pinned FFmpeg download, **Firebase Realtime
Database** (project `theia-friends`) for friend codes, profile photos, requests,
presence, opinions and rooms, and **Google Drive** only when you connect it with
your own Google client. Remote access passively accepts encrypted WireGuard
packets from peers you configure; it never contacts a control plane, relay or
discovery service. The Android application talks only to your own Theia server. No telemetry is sent,
and the library catalogue and viewing history are not uploaded.

Theia is a personal media server: one user, their own films, their own machine.
See the privacy page of the site for what leaves the computer.
