# Theia 4.4.4 — the end of an episode, out of your way

4.4.4 refines what appears when an episode opens and ends. The countdown no
longer draws over the timeline, *Skip opening* no longer stays on the picture
for the whole opening, and the buttons have a new look. It also carries the
4.4.3 repair, and below it everything 4.4.2 brought, so this page is the whole
story of the end of V4.

The server, library, ordinary viewing and all features in this release remain
free for noncommercial use. There is no cloud account or telemetry.

## Updating to 4.4.4

**From 4.4.2 or 4.4.3:** this is a **product release**, the native player does
not change. Update from Theia's own update button - *Settings → Updates*, then
**Check for updates**. The server is replaced, and the signed interface pack is
checked, kept, and applied the next time the player starts. You do not need to
run a setup again.

**From 4.4.1 or older: run the 4.4.4 setup once.** 4.4.2 improved the native
player itself, and its interface refuses to start on the player 4.4.0 and 4.4.1
installed; a server-only update does not replace the player. The setup carries
the 4.4.2 player and this release's interface. Your library, profiles,
watchlists, preferences and viewing history stay in place, and so do the
settings you already saved.

**Both of you need 4.4.2 or later** for what two households share: a private
room, the presence choices, and a friendship removed on both sides. With a
friend still on an older version, basic presence keeps working. A private room
needs every participant on 4.4.2 or later: an older server refuses to create or
join one rather than opening a room anybody could enter.

**Player updates are your choice.** The player does not check for updates on its
own: open *Settings → Updates* and use **Check for updates**, or switch on
**Automatic updates** to have the player look when it starts, download in the
background while you browse, and offer to restart. A server installed alone
still updates itself, as before, and never during a film.

## What changes in 4.4.4

- **The timeline comes first.** Moving the pointer onto the playback bar near the
  end of an episode used to paint the scene preview and the countdown over each
  other. Now, while you are on the timeline, the end-of-episode buttons step
  aside, almost transparent, and let the pointer through; the countdown waits
  for you and carries on when you leave the bar, with at least five seconds to
  read it. In a watch party the countdown belongs to the whole room, so there
  the buttons step aside but the room's countdown keeps running.
- **Skip opening leaves by itself.** It shows for eight seconds when the opening
  starts. If you let the opening play, it leaves the picture, and it comes back
  whenever you move the mouse to show the controls. *Go to the post-credits
  scene* behaves the same way. The countdown and the end-of-season sentence stay
  until you answer them.
- **New buttons.** *Skip opening* is a dark pill with an arrow that sweeps
  across it when you point at it. *Next episode* is a light pill that fills from
  left to right as the 30-second countdown runs, instead of a sentence above it
  (screen readers still hear the seconds). *Keep watching* sits beside it,
  quieter. The panel behind them is gone.

Nothing else changes: no library scan, playback engine, native contract,
Firebase rule or settings behaviour is touched.

## Also included: 4.4.3, the interface follows the update

- **A tester build is older than its release.** The interface compared versions
  by their three numbers alone, so `4.4.2-test.3` counted as `4.4.2`. It now
  follows the same order as the setup and the shell: a prerelease ranks below its
  release, and build metadata is ignored. The update button no longer mistakes a
  finished update for a current one, and no longer skips the setup a shell
  release needs.
- **The interface travels as a pack again.** 4.4.2 was a shell release and
  published no pack, so an installation that had confirmed a tester pack had
  nothing newer to fetch. 4.4.3 published the signed pack, and 4.4.4 does too.

## Downloads

4.4.4 publishes the six standalone servers, the setups for **Windows x64, Linux
x64, macOS Apple Silicon and macOS Intel** around the verified 4.4.2 player, and
the signed interface pack with its signature. Each setup carries the server,
launcher, native player and media engine. The Windows ARM64 and Ubuntu ARM64
setups are not part of this release: the website links their last verified
setup, **4.2.1**, which carries the player of before 4.4 and does not have these
features. Their 4.4 setups are planned for early November, once their native
checks can run.

## What was and was not checked in 4.4.4

- The three changes were exercised in a browser, on the interface's own
  components: the buttons stepping aside for the timeline and the countdown
  waiting, then resuming; *Skip opening* leaving after eight seconds and coming
  back with the controls; the fill of *Next episode*; and every earlier case of
  the episode flow (cancel, seek back, season end, sleep timer, rooms).
- They have **not** been tried on a real episode in the native player before
  publication.

## In the player (since 4.4.2)

- **A timeline you can read.** Move the pointer along the playback bar and a
  still of the scene at that second follows it, with its time. Nothing seeks
  until you click. The picture is the exact frame, not the nearest keyframe,
  decoded by a second, paused copy of the player's own engine; no thumbnail files
  are written. A position nobody has visited yet still takes a moment.
- **Skip arrows that say what they do, and keep up.** The step is **30 seconds**
  by default and shown on both buttons (**−30 s / +30 s**); *Settings → Playback*
  offers 10, 30 or 60. Pressing them repeatedly adds up and reaches the position
  you meant, one jump at a time.
- **Episodes that follow on.** When a file marks where its opening, its ending or
  a scene after the credits begin, the player offers **Skip opening** (never
  automatic), then a quiet **Next episode** countdown of 30 seconds that you can
  cancel, and **Go to the post-credits scene** when there is one. It stops at the
  last episode of a season and says so; it never counts down into the next season
  by itself. This reads the **chapter names written in the file**. It does not
  guess from the picture or the sound, so a file without such chapters shows
  nothing extra and keeps the Next episode button it always had.
- **A sleep timer:** stop after this episode or after a chosen time.
- **Settings in eight places, nothing lost.** General, Playback, Audio &
  subtitles, Privacy, Server, Updates, Statistics and Help. New: the skip step,
  what to do when you return to a title (resume, ask or restart, with an optional
  short rewind), audio and subtitle choices remembered **per series** with your
  profile's defaults behind them, ordered language preferences, forced-only
  subtitles, a preference for or against hearing-impaired tracks, the audio output
  device, audio and subtitle timing, fullscreen at the start, whether minimising
  pauses, interface scale apart from subtitle size, the size of the artwork and
  preview caches with a button to clear them (media, profiles, opinions and
  history are never touched), and keyboard shortcuts you can change, with a check
  for conflicts. Every section can be reset on its own.
- **A track menu with more room.** The Synchronisation tab is gone; its sliders
  live in *Settings → Audio & subtitles*.
- **A window that closes.** Some players reported a Close button that did
  nothing after a film. Closing, from its own button or from the system's close
  action, now follows a bounded path that saves your progress and then exits,
  without having to end the task.
- **Spoiler protection on a series page.** An outlined **Spoiler protection**
  toggle blurs the pictures of the episodes you have not finished and turns off
  their previews. It starts off each time you open the series and is not saved or
  shared. Titles and synopses are not hidden.
- **A calmer library screen.** The strip above the Search navigation is gone, the
  artwork fades continuously beneath the window controls, the navigation bar has
  more room for your profile photo and its presence ring, and a card's preview no
  longer draws a light arc in its left corners. Your own photo now shows in the
  profile list too.

## Friends and rooms (since 4.4.2)

- **Presence you can read.** The library bar shows how many friends are around
  with their faces beside the number. A friend is *Online*, *Inactive* (after
  2, 5 or 10 minutes, as you choose), *Watching*, or *Offline*, with a mark on
  the photo that does not rely on colour alone. You can choose for a while to be
  **Up for a film**, **Do not disturb** or **Invisible**, hide your title or your
  last-seen time, and ask for a quiet notice when a chosen friend comes back.
  Presence belongs to an open player, not to a server that happens to be on, and
  pausing a film is never announced.
- **Ending a friendship ends it on both sides,** even if the other server was off
  at the time.
- **Rooms are private.** A new room admits only the friends the host invites. An
  invitation is for one profile, lasts until the room ends and survives a
  reconnection. The host can also let others *ask* to join and answer each
  request. Guests cannot invite anybody. Invitations are shown to their recipient
  and nobody else.

## What Firebase now holds

Theia's Firebase database still holds directory entries for all local profiles,
including names and pictures, and carries friend requests, presence and the
opinions you write between linked friends, under anonymous server identities.
**New in 4.4.2:** your chosen availability, a generic activity (browsing, in a
room, watching) and, if you leave it on, the last moment your player was seen
reach each friend who can read your presence. Only that last moment is kept,
never a history. *Invisible* publishes no activity, title, room offer or last
seen, and a hidden title hides its artwork and episode details too. Ending a
friendship places an authenticated removal notice in the other friend's inbox
until their server has processed it. Your library catalogue and viewing history are still not
sent, and no sign-up is required. The database rules gained exactly the
validators this needs and were deployed on 20 October 2026, before this release.

## Platform limits

macOS apps are ad-hoc signed and not notarised: open the app the first time with
Control-click → Open. Linux playback remains software-decoded SDR with PCM
output. Windows binaries are unsigned and may show a reputation warning. Rooms
need direct connectivity through the configured encrypted tunnel; there is no
third-party relay.

## What was and was not checked in 4.4.2

- The timeline preview, the settings, spoiler protection, presence, a private
  room's island, the profile photo and closing were run in the real Windows
  player, on the maintainer's machine. The skip buttons and the episode
  countdown were exercised in browser and unit tests, not on a real episode
  ending. macOS and Linux are checked by the release pipeline's installation and
  generated-film tests; hardware decoding, HDR output and a physical Intel Mac
  are not. The exact timeline preview and the list of audio outputs were also run
  against the engine the Linux setup ships. On Linux the instant preview applies to
  SDR films; for an HDR film it comes from the server, as in 4.4.1, because that
  engine cannot tone-map a still. This has not been measured on macOS.
- Two problems reported earlier, the freeze after pressing skip repeatedly and a
  window that would not close, were **never reproduced**. The fixes are reasoned,
  covered by tests and by a native close test that starts from a healthy state.
- The new presence was tried between two households on a test build and reported
  working. Private rooms, their invitations and the shared countdown have been
  checked with isolated servers on one machine, not yet between two real
  households.
- Opening and ending markers come only from chapters named in the file; how many
  real libraries carry them has not been measured.

## Not in 4.4.2

A few ideas were proposed during this release and not retained: correcting a
detected marker by hand, searching the settings, hiding episode titles and
synopses, undoing a seek, browsing a file's chapters, checking that the next
episode exists before counting down, and explaining a track fallback. None has a
date.

## After 4.4.4

4.4.2 was the last feature update of V4, and the next step is **Theia 5.0**.
Where V4 was about people watching together, 5.0 looks outward: everything that
connects Theia to what surrounds it, such as cloud and servers, and storage in
the cloud. Whether it also opens Theia to mods or plugins is not decided. There
is no date, and nothing is promised beyond this.

Two things are certain. **There will be no account and no telemetry,** in 5.0 as
today. And **for now every feature of V4 stays free**: if an optional extra is ever
offered, it will be announced well in advance, and the server, the library and
ordinary viewing will stay free.
