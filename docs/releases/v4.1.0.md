# Theia 4.1 — watch parties

4.0 let you see what your friends were watching. 4.1 lets you watch it with
them: same film, same second, in your living room and theirs. I tested it with a
friend in another household before writing this, and the two screens stayed
together to the frame.

**4.1 is free**, like 4.0: the server, the library, ordinary viewing and every
new feature in this release cost nothing.

**Upgrading:** run the new setup. It finds your installation, closes the running
programs, replaces them and starts the server again; your library, progress,
profiles and friends stay where they were. **Both of you need 4.1 to watch
together**, and a friend code made by 4.1 can carry an IPv6 address that a 4.0
server does not read, so ask your friends to update too.

On macOS and Linux, make the setup executable (`chmod +x` and its filename),
then run it. Mac apps are ad-hoc signed and not notarised, so macOS may ask you
to allow the app in Privacy & Security on first launch. Windows binaries are
unsigned and may show a reputation warning. Ubuntu still needs
`libwebkit2gtk-4.1-0` and `libayatana-appindicator3-1`.

## What is new

### Watch parties

- **Open a room from the film you are watching.** Your friends see it on your
  card in their Friends sheet within twenty seconds, and join in one press.
- **Everyone plays their own copy**, from their own server, and only the
  commands travel: play, pause, seek. The player keeps everyone within a
  fraction of a second by nudging its speed, and jumps only when it is far off.
  When somebody's film is still loading, the room waits for them, ten seconds
  at most.
- **No copy? You can still join.** The host's server sends its file to the
  friends who do not have the film, straight through the encrypted tunnel, and
  the host can turn that off with one switch. It uses the host's upload, so the
  picture is as good as their connection allows.
- **The host decides who controls playback**: only them by default, or everyone,
  with one switch. Each guest has their own offset, for a copy that is a second
  or two longer than the host's.
- **Up to eight people per room**, on the same library or in other households.
  A room lives in the host's server's memory and disappears with it; nothing
  about it is written down.

### The island

A black pill under the title bar that changes shape to tell you what is
happening. Outside a room it shows when the film will end. In a room it shows the
faces of who is there, with their profile pictures, and opens into the room
itself: who is loading, the host's switches, your offset, the way out. It grows
when somebody arrives, leaves or is being waited for, even while the controls are
hidden, and otherwise comes and goes with them.

### Friends

- **How many friends are online** is shown on the Friends entry of the library
  bar.
- **Remote access in one press.** Theia now asks your router from every network
  interface, lets the router choose the port when the usual one is taken, and
  puts your IPv6 address in your friend code, so most boxes need no setting at
  all. The hand-typed router address is still there, folded away, for the rest.
- **Versions are checked.** Friends stay visible online across versions, but a
  room is only possible when both sides speak the same room protocol: the
  Friends sheet tells you which side needs to update, with a button to do it.

### Everyday

- *Who's watching?* is asked even with a single profile, and a profile can be
  created or edited from that screen and from the profile sheet.
- Closing the player now closes the server that was started for it, after ten
  seconds, so nothing stays behind in the task manager. A server you started on
  purpose (`theia server`, or at Windows startup) stays running.
- The library bar gives up its labels for icons when the window is too narrow
  for them.

## Known limits

- Rooms were tested between two Windows households. macOS and Ubuntu build and
  pass their checks, but a room on them has not been watched by a person yet.
- The film a host sends is not converted: a guest needs the bandwidth of the
  original file, and a player able to decode it.
- Friends and rooms between households still need either a router that answers
  UPnP or NAT-PMP, IPv6 on both sides, or a port opened by hand. Theia never uses
  a relay or a third-party server.

## The detailed list

**Player**
- Watch parties: create, join, leave; drift correction by speed nudge; waits for
  a loading member; personal offset; join without a copy (decision 176).
- The island, replacing the room panel and its notices.
- Member pictures in the room, served by the host and re-encoded by each server.
- Online friends count; version warning in the Friends sheet; a refused join is
  explained beside the button that was pressed.
- *Who's watching?* with one profile; profiles created from the sheet (165).

**Server and network**
- Rooms held in memory by the host's server, reached through the friends-only
  tunnel; every request checked against the friendship and the member's token.
- Room protocol announced in presence and on every room request; mismatched
  joins refused (`room_version_mismatch`).
- Router mapping from every interface, router-chosen external port, NAT-PMP,
  stable IPv6 in friend codes, endpoint rotation when a friend's address moves
  (178).
- A server started by `theia` for the player stops once no player has run for
  ten seconds (139).
